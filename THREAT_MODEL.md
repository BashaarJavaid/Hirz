# Threat Model

What Hirz protects against, what it explicitly does not, and the assumptions the design rests on. See [`ARCHITECTURE.md`](./ARCHITECTURE.md) for how each protection is implemented and [`docs/adr/`](./docs/adr/) for why alternatives were rejected.

An explicit scope boundary is worth more to a technical reviewer than an implied claim of total coverage. **Rule for this file:** every row reads **Planned** until the code that earns it exists and its `verify:` check in `ROADMAP.md` passes; only then does it become the **Yes** or **Partial** it names. "No" rows are design decisions and need no code. Claims never outrun the implementation.

Item 9 earns only the internal durable-grant protections below, backed by
[the verification evidence](./docs/verification-log.md#item-9--complete-2026-09-18).
A grant is not device execution (item 19). Public authentication, physical execution,
AWS enforcement and audit anchoring retain their pending claims. Item 10 earns the
Partial audit-verification claim below, backed by
[its evidence](./docs/verification-log.md#item-10--complete-2026-09-18).

## Threats

| Threat | Protected? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hirz takes an action the household marked `never` | **Yes for internal grants** (item 9) | Explicit class/role `never` is terminal before scoring and compiles to a Cedar `forbid`. Risk-dependent NEVER also wins, using current supplied facts. Both evaluators must permit before a durable grant; the explicit class/role veto requires no observation facts |
| Hirz takes a high-risk action without asking | **Yes for internal grants** (item 9) | Risk floors are code: HIGH → ASK minimum, CRITICAL → never autonomous. A constitution cannot loosen a floor; validation rejects over-broad `auto`. Public activation remains pending |
| Approval reused for a different action (TOCTOU) | **Yes for internal grants** (item 9) | Approvals bind to the action `content_hash`; redemption re-runs the pipeline against current state; mismatch → `DENY_APPROVAL_MISMATCH`; a newer doorbell press also mismatches; expiry → `DENY_APPROVAL_EXPIRED` |
| Approval granted under conditions that no longer hold (someone fell asleep, a guest arrived) | **Planned** (Phase 3; will be Yes) | Execution-time re-evaluation; a changed context turns execute into ask and revises the plan |
| Prompt injection through utterances, calendar titles, contact names, or scenario text | **Planned** (Phase 3; will be Partial) | Model output never decides anything: the pipeline, risk engine, constitution, and executor are code. Injected text can at most distort an explanation or a Protect signal extraction, both of which are schema-validated and, for Protect, weighted by code. What Hirz cannot control is Alexa+'s own orchestrator being manipulated into calling the wrong tool with the wrong arguments; the pipeline then treats that call like any other request from that member |
| Family-impersonation and organization-impersonation scams relayed by a member (Mom: "Malik called from a strange number and needs money. Is it really him?") | **Planned** (Phase 6; will be Partial) | Hirz verifies against stored verified channels and the subject's own app, never against caller-supplied facts, and has no way to move money (no payment adapter exists, and no money action is offered to the orchestrator). Hirz cannot see the phone call itself, never claims anything about the caller's number unless the member reads it out (and then only "matches your saved record" or "does not", since caller ID can be forged), and depends on the member asking; the contact is asked about the specific request, and a "yes, that was me" is never an endorsement of paying. A model may add signals to the keyword extractor's and can never remove one; a member who acts without asking Hirz is outside its reach. Hirz gives the member one thing to do, ask first; it does not stop scam calls |
| Someone arrives to collect after a scam call (courier pickup) | **Planned** (Phase 6; will be Partial) | A recent CRITICAL verification case plus an unexpected visitor at the same door triggers a warning and a notification to the contact Hirz verified. Hirz only knows about calls the member reported, and it never claims the visitor is the scammer |
| Unlock for an unexpected visitor | **Planned** (Phase 6; will be Yes, when the household's constitution says so) | `never_for: [unexpected_visitor]` is a rule the household writes, not a built-in floor; once written it is a hard veto. Without it an unexpected visitor is still an `ask` on a phone, never by voice. "Unexpected" means a press that matches no expected arrival window; it says nothing about who the person is. Expected-visitor context comes from the schedule and the household graph, never from video identification, and it never identifies anyone: a stranger who rings inside Mom's window gets the same phone approval Mom would, worded "Someone is at the front door. Mom is expected now.", and the member who approves is the identification (`scenarios/stranger-in-window.yaml`). If the doorbell is offline, `state_stale` raises the band |
| A rule change spoken to a shared Echo (a guest loosening the rules, or anyone tightening them) | **Planned** (Phase 4; will be Yes) | `propose_household_rule` only records a proposal. Drafting happens in the worker, and activation happens in the companion app under a passkey, attributable to one person. A voice never activates, loosens, or tightens anything |
| A bug or a bypass path in Hirz's own processes acts on a home device without a boundary decision | **Planned** (Phase 7; will be Yes for devices behind Hirz Link in AWS mode) | The Home Assistant token never leaves the house. Hirz Link executes only commands signed by a KMS key that only the Gateway's Lambda role can use, and that Lambda signs only what the policy engine permitted. The worker can delay or drop a command, never forge one. The signing Lambda recomputes the action hash instead of trusting the worker's, so an approved lights action cannot be presented as an unlock. This row is about bugs and bypass paths, not a compromised worker (two rows down). Local mode has no such boundary and says so (`boundary.engine: dogwood-local`) |
| A forged, tampered, expired, replayed, redirected, or repeated device command | **Planned** (Phase 7; will be Yes) | Signature over action id, home id, class, target, params, content hash, expiry (≤ 60 s), nonce, and any revert; Link refuses a command addressed to another home and a second command for an operation it already executed, so one approval yields at most one operation; everything refused is reported and audited as `LINK_REJECTED` |
| A compromised worker claims an approval that never happened, or lies about context facts | **No** today. **Planned** for `security.*` approvals (Phase 7, item 38d; will be Partial) | The worker is the process that tells the boundary an approval was given, and it supplies the requester's role and the context facts, so a fully compromised worker can obtain a signature for anything the constitution would allow after an approval. Item 38d moves the proof for `security.*` classes to the member's passkey: the assertion, over the action hash, is verified inside the signing Lambda against keys the worker cannot write (ADR-010). It will be Partial, not Yes, because the approval page is still served by Hirz and could show one thing while asking the passkey to sign another. `auto` classes stay exposed to false facts by design (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §3.3) |
| The door stays unlocked because the cloud failed after the unlock (internet, Postgres, or the worker lost before the relock) | **Planned** (Phase 7; will be Partial) | A bounded operation is authorized whole: the signed command carries its revert, Hirz Link stores it durably before actuating and runs it from its own clock, offline and across a restart; a device-native auto-relock is used too where there is one. Partial because a lock that refuses to relock can only be reported (`VERIFY_FAILED`, notification), never guaranteed |
| The same device is operated through a path that is not Hirz (linked to Alexa directly, exposed to an assistant by Home Assistant, the vendor's app, a key) | **No**; detected after the fact where Hirz can see state | Hirz governs the actions Hirz takes. A refusal by Hirz does not stop another integration from doing the same thing, and no API lets Hirz discover every other path. The deployment rule is that a governed device is not exposed to Alexa by another route; the Household page shows each device as *managed through Hirz* or *not managed*, and says nothing it cannot know about other paths. A state change on a governed device with no matching Hirz command is audited as `OUT_OF_BAND_CHANGE`, with a notification for locks and cameras |
| A bypass reaches a cloud adapter (EV, calendar) with a write-capable credential | **Planned** (Phase 7; will be Partial) | IAM, not convention: write-capable credentials are readable by the `hirz-actions` Lambda's role only; the worker's role is denied. There is no signed-command check on the vendor's side, so this row is Partial |
| A visitor to the public hosted demo reaches a real device | **Planned** (Phase 7; will be Yes) | Demo households can bind only `twin` adapters; the registry refuses anything else for them and a test asserts it; demo households expire after 24 hours |
| Cross-household data access with a valid token | **Planned** (Phase 4; will be Yes) | Every query is scoped by the `household_id` derived from the token; a two-household isolation test runs in CI |
| Stolen or leaked access token | **Planned** (Phase 4; will be Partial) | Short-lived JWTs, `aud`-bound, refresh handled by the AS; the holder still gets the linked member's authority until expiry. Security classes cannot be approved with the token alone (approval is in the companion app under a passkey), and a claimed role only lowers authority. Revocation is the AS's job |
| A child, guest, or visitor speaking to a shared Echo with the owner's linked account | **Planned** (Phase 1; will be Partial) | Alexa gives add-ons no speaker identity, so roles cannot be enforced by voice and Hirz does not pretend to. Security classes are never approvable by voice (constitution principle 6; validator-enforced), so the worst a voice can do is what the linked account may do *autonomously* under the constitution; the household can tighten that further with `requester.surface` conditions. Non-security `auto` classes remain reachable by anyone in the room, which is the household's explicit choice |
| Forged Ring doorbell event | **Planned** (Phase 6; will be Yes) | HMAC-SHA256 signature verification with a replay window; unsigned or replayed events are dropped and audited |
| Tampered audit trail | **Partial** (item 10; local verification; anchors pending item 38b) | SHA-256 chain and per-row ECDSA P-256 signatures; database verification checks a consistent snapshot and pointer; exported intervals verify offline against an independently trusted key/fingerprint and expected household. Changed rows, missing internal links and pointer mismatches are detected. A tail removed together with resetting its pointer, complete erasure, or history rewritten and re-signed by the worker cannot be excluded. Partial exports prove only included signatures and internal links; their unsigned wrapper cannot attest completeness. Results explicitly say "not anchored: local mode". S3 Object Lock checkpoints and `--anchors` remain unimplemented; no AWS protection is earned here |
| Silent divergence between what the household approved and what was enforced | **Planned** (Phase 7; will be Partial) | In AWS mode; locally stage 7 is a second evaluator in the same trust domain and is labeled as one. Boundary agreement (stage 7) records both engines' decisions; disagreement fails closed and is audited; the conformance suite runs both engines over the corpus. The boundary independently evaluates action class, role, parameter bounds, action hash, and approval history from the request alone. Context facts (occupancy, sleeping, quiet hours) are supplied by Hirz from the pipeline's snapshot, hash-bound so the audit row proves both engines saw the same facts; a wrong snapshot is caught by verify-after-act and the `state_stale` factor, not by the boundary. Two engines agreeing on one compiled text does not test the compiler, so a property-based test compares the YAML evaluator with the compiled policy over generated cases. Policies are scoped per household, and a short-TTL approval cannot ride a longer permit (`docs/constitution.md` §4) |
| Constitution authoring mistakes (always-allow, never-satisfiable, over-broad) | **Planned** (Phase 7; will be Partial) | Tighten-only, risk floors, and the security-never-by-voice rule reject dangerous constitutions everywhere. AgentCore Policy's automated reasoning additionally rejects always-allow and never-satisfiable policies in AWS mode; local activation records that this analysis did not run. Analysis cannot know the household's intent; a rule that is valid and permissive is the household's decision |
| Physical harm from an unsafe setpoint or an open lock | **Planned** (Phase 3; will be Partial) | Code-level clamps (HVAC range, maximum unlock and camera-off windows) apply regardless of policy. Hirz does not control life-safety systems and must not be connected to them |
| A twin that lies about the world (stale or wrong observations) | **Planned** (Phase 3; will be Partial) | Observations carry freshness; stale state raises the risk band; verify-after-act compares expected and observed. A twin is not a sensor; real deployments should use real adapters for anything safety-relevant. A real device that goes unreachable is reported as `unavailable; actual state unknown` and is never replaced by its twin outside a scenario or demo household; a twin read-back never verifies a real device |
| Unauthorized "learning" of preferences from conversations | **Partial** (item 20; locally verified backend enforcement) | Temperature proposals reach graph/planner inputs only after the subject's app-principal consent, with atomic refresh and signed evidence; learning `never` blocks creation and acceptance. Authenticated companion consent remains unverified. [Contract](./ARCHITECTURE.md#59-memory), [evidence](./docs/verification-log.md#item-20--2026-09-21) |
| Model provider outage | **Planned** (Phase 3; will be Yes) | Decisions never depend on the model; explanations and drafting degrade to templates and forms |
| Compromised Hirz host or AWS account | **No** | The signing key, the database, and the credential vault are then lost; this is infrastructure hardening, not application logic |
| What Hirz says aloud on a shared Echo (who is home, the schedule) | **Partial, by design** | Anyone in the room can ask a linked Echo what the linked account may read. Channel values, safe words, and verification history are never spoken or returned by any tool; a household-written disclosure policy is not in v1 (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §14). Across homes nothing is shared by default: a trusted contact has no login in the other household and learns only what that household's own rules send them (a check-in, a courier warning) |
| Malicious linked adult | **No** | Attributable after the fact through the audit trail; not prevented. Quorum rules limit what one adult can approve alone for classes that require `all_adults` |
| Face or voice identification of people | **No, by design** | Hirz never identifies people from video or audio and never claims to. Requester identity is the linked account plus a *claimed* role; visitor identity is schedule context. If Alexa ever passes a recognized-speaker identifier to add-ons, Hirz will accept it only as a hint that lowers or matches authority, never as one that raises it |
| Autonomous financial transfers | **No, by design** | No payment adapter exists or is planned; the classes exist only so the constitution can forbid them provably |

## Assumptions

- The household's authorization server (Cognito in AWS, the dev issuer locally) is correctly configured with PKCE S256, short token lifetimes, and a resource indicator bound to Hirz. Hirz does not defend against a misconfigured or compromised authorization server.
- Alexa+ delivers the linked account's access token faithfully and does not expose voice-identity claims to add-ons; Hirz's identity model assumes only what the token proves. An Echo is treated as a shared device: whoever is in the room holds the linked account's authority, which is why security approvals happen in the companion app and not by voice.
- The home running Hirz Link is physically and administratively the household's: whoever controls that machine controls the Home Assistant token, exactly as they would without Hirz.
- The KMS signing key's policy is as deployed by the CDK stack (one Lambda role may sign); an AWS administrator can change it, which is the "Compromised AWS account" row.
- Home Assistant, Smartcar, Ring, ComEd, and Open-Meteo report state faithfully; Hirz defends against stale and missing data, not against a lying upstream.
- The Postgres instance and the audit signing key are managed by the operator. With an independently trusted public key, Hirz detects stored-row mutations and broken links, not tail-and-pointer rollback, complete erasure, or changes re-signed by a compromised worker. A database superuser and loss of the signing key remain outside the protection; independent anchors are still pending.
- AgentCore Policy evaluates the compiled Cedar/Dogwood faithfully; the local Dogwood evaluator is a conformance check on Hirz's compiler and the local-mode boundary, not a second opinion on AWS.
- The digital twin is labeled as such in every surface, and no safety-relevant decision in a real home relies on twin data.
- Hirz is not a medical device, not a life-safety system, and not a financial institution. It executes comfort preferences, energy schedules, and household coordination within a written boundary, and it verifies identity from records the household established in advance.
